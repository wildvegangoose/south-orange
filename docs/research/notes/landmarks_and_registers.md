# South Orange, NJ: Historic Landmarks, Historic Districts and Register Listings

Scope: Township/Village of South Orange only (Essex County, NJ). Maplewood landmarks are excluded. Where Maplewood confusion is possible, it is flagged. "NR" = National Register of Historic Places; "SR"/"NJRHP" = New Jersey Register of Historic Places; "NRIS" = National Register reference number; "SHPO ID#" = NJ Historic Preservation Office ID number.

Method note: Primary sources were downloaded and read in full or in part: seven NPS nomination PDFs from NPGallery (read with pdftotext), the Village's 2016 historic preservation ordinance with its Schedule A list, the HPC's 2014 historic timeline, the HPC's 2024 FAQ, the HPC's 2014 letter on Village Hall, the Village's 2007 Historic Districts map, and South Orange Public Library (SOPL) local-history wiki pages. The NJ HPO county list (ESSEX.pdf) could not be retrieved: nj.gov returned 403/404 and archive.org was unreachable. NJ Register dates and ID numbers therefore come from Wikipedia infoboxes, which cite that list, and from the Village ordinance. Some Seton Hall and church facts come only from search-result summaries, because blogs.shu.edu returned 503/401. These are marked "[search-snippet only]".

## 1. National Register (and NJ Register) listings in South Orange: dates, NRIS numbers, NJ Register data

### Takeaway
South Orange has exactly seven NR listings: Eugene V. Kelly Carriage House (1975), South Orange Village Hall (1976), South Orange Station and Mountain Station (both 1984, under the Operating Passenger Railroad Stations Thematic Resource), the Stone House by the Stone House Brook / Old Stone House (1991), Montrose Park Historic District (1997) and the South Orange Fire Department (1998). No South Orange listing has been delisted. There is no NR-listed "Prospect Street Historic District": it holds only a SHPO Opinion. No Seton Hall building other than the Kelly Carriage House is NR-listed.

**1a. Eugene V. Kelly Carriage House (now Father Vincent Monella Art Center, Seton Hall University)**
- Address: "S. Orange Ave., Seton Hall University campus". No street number is given in the NR record — [NPGallery NRIS 75001136](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/75001136)
- NR listed 11/10/1975; architect "Baker, John E."; significant year 1887; significant name Kelly, Eugene; criteria Architecture/Engineering and Person; style Late Victorian — [NPGallery NRIS 75001136](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/75001136)
- NJ Register October 3, 1980, NJRHP #1360 (Wikipedia infobox citing the NJ HPO Essex list). The NJ date falls after the NR date — [Wikipedia: Eugene V. Kelly Carriage House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_V._Kelly_Carriage_House)
- Nomination text: Kelly, "a wealthy banker from New York, bought the property in 1867, and years later, in 1887 made extensive improvements, enlarging the house (now demolished) and updating it in the Queen Anne style". He moved the existing barns and built a brick carriage house designed by local architect John E. Baker. The building has terra cotta panels, a "romantic stair turret", and originally had mahogany horse stalls with brass name plates and grooms' rooms upstairs — [NR nomination, Kelly Carriage House (NPGallery PDF)](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6b724602-5940-4970-807f-05c2e049c9a5)
- Conversion to the Art Center was "planned and directed by Dr. Louis de Foix-Crenascol and Richard J. Gascoyne, architect". The work added a glass entry wall 10 ft inside the main arch, closed the gallery windows and added a new upper ceiling. The form was prepared by Mrs. Lou H. Mitchell, Seton Hall, dated 7/25/75 — [NR nomination, Kelly Carriage House](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6b724602-5940-4970-807f-05c2e049c9a5)
- Kelly biography from the nomination: born in Trillick, County Tyrone, Ireland; dry goods and banking in San Francisco from 1850; founded a bank in San Francisco and New York in 1861. He married Margaret Hughes, niece of Archbishop John Hughes. He was a Seton Hall trustee and patron from 1879, a founder of the Catholic University of America, a Metropolitan Museum trustee, and treasurer of the Irish Parliamentary Fund. Chain of title runs Jepthis Baldwin to Aaron Baldwin to Eugene Kelly, then "Kelly's sons to Seton Hall College" — [NR nomination, Kelly Carriage House](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6b724602-5940-4970-807f-05c2e049c9a5)
- Seton Hall purchased "the Kelly estate, east of the present campus ... for the bishop's residence in 1901" — [Montrose Park HD nomination, Sec. 8](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Bibliography sources cited in the nomination: South Orange Bulletin, September 10, 1887 (construction of the new carriage house) and June 11, 1891 (a Baker advertisement listing the Kelly home) — [NR nomination, Kelly Carriage House](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6b724602-5940-4970-807f-05c2e049c9a5)

**1b. South Orange Village Hall**
- NR 76001152, listed 5/28/1976; NR location "S. Orange Ave. and Scotland Rd."; architects "Rossiter & Wright"; significant year 1894 — [NPGallery NRIS 76001152](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/76001152)
- NJ Register December 8, 1975, NJRHP #1363 — [Wikipedia: South Orange Village Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange_Village_Hall)
- Street address today: 101 South Orange Avenue. The Village's municipal offices are now at 76 South Orange Avenue — [Village of South Orange: 101 South Orange Avenue: Village Hall](https://southorange.org/856/101-South-Orange-Avenue-Village-Hall)
- The nomination was prepared by Charles Tichy (restoration architect) and Terry Karschner (historian-curator), NJ DEP Historic Sites Section, March 12, 1975. It describes brick first-story walls with stucco and half-timber upper levels, a slate roof, and an "octagonal clock tower ... topped by a copper dome". A 1906 postcard shows "the original cupola with bell atop the dome". The only exterior alteration noted is "the enclosure of the three arches at the main entrance". The nomination suggests the firm was that of Ehrick K. Rossiter (1854-1941) and cites the New York Times, October 14, 1894, p. 24 — [NR nomination, Village Hall (NPGallery PDF)](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/7b7019c2-f7e2-43db-aec9-ce246907eaca)
- Construction history, from a c.1975 essay by Clare Rex Wiesman and Arthur Klimowicz:
  - On December 15, 1892, Trustee Timothy Barrett proposed $5,000 in bonds.
  - In February 1894 the Trustees picked a Presbyterian-owned site at Prospect and Irvington Avenues. A March 13, 1894 hearing rejected it, with 90% of citizens opposed.
  - On May 21, 1894 the Roth property at Scotland Street and South Orange Avenue was recommended at $6,000. The lot purchase was decided June 18, 1894, and $11,000 was warranted June 25.
  - Final plans were accepted July 23, 1894, and contracts awarded August 6, 1894 for $12,171. "Mr. Wright was a resident of South Orange."
  - The bell was placed January 17, 1895. The hall opened at the Trustees' March meeting on March 18, 1895, with Edward Self giving the dedication address. A box of documents sits behind the bronze tablet.
  - The fire department stayed until 1930. The belfry was possibly removed then and the 1,000-lb bell "disappeared". Police and court moved out in 1972.
  - [SOPL Local History wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History)
- American Architect and Building News of January 20, 1894 published a rendering and plans: "50 foot tower ... surmounted by a belfry, beneath which the fire hose may be hung to dry" — quoted by Naoma Welk, Images of America: South Orange, in [HPC letter to Trustees, Sept. 2, 2014](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/HPC-Ltr-to-Trustees.pdf)
- Local legend holds that the Village Hall clock inspired The Secret of the Old Clock (1930), the first Nancy Drew book, from Edward Stratemeyer of neighboring Maplewood. This is presented as legend — [HPC letter, 2014](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/HPC-Ltr-to-Trustees.pdf); [SOPL wiki: Village Hall](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall)
- Adaptive reuse:
  - Sold in 2016 to Landmark Hospitality Group for $1,200,000, with a stated investment of $6,500,000 and a 20-year PILOT on improvements — [Village: 101 South Orange Avenue](https://southorange.org/856/101-South-Orange-Avenue-Village-Hall)
  - The restaurant and beer garden opened November 2022. It reopened February 26, 2025 as "Felina Steak South Orange"; the beer garden became "La Terrazza Bar". Landmark Hospitality still owns the building — [Village Green, Feb. 20, 2025](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=108970)
  - The HPC's September 2, 2014 letter, signed by Chairman Gary Hill, urged the Trustees to keep the building in Village ownership — [HPC letter](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/HPC-Ltr-to-Trustees.pdf)

**1c. South Orange Station (NJ Transit)**
- NR 84002669, listed 6/22/1984, part of the "Operating Passenger Railroad Stations TR". Address "19 Sloan St."; architect "Nies, Frank J."; style Renaissance; significant year 1916 — [NPGallery NRIS 84002669](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/84002669)
- Address conflict: the NR record says 19 Sloan St. — [NPGallery](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/84002669). Wikipedia's station infobox says "17 Sloan Street" — [Wikipedia: South Orange station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange_station)
- Survey form:
  - "Architect Frank J. Nies; Date 1916; Style Renaissance Revival with 'Prairie style' elements"
  - Trains per day: 80 in 1980; peak 112 in 1940; 87 in 1901 at the "Original station on site"
  - "The complex is the first to have been elevated on cylindrical concrete piers", and it predates the East Orange and Brick Church stations
  - The large east-facade window "interacts with the similarly detailed firestation to the S."
  - [NR nomination/survey, South Orange Station (NPGallery PDF)](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/9a4e511d-181e-4493-80cd-21d265a8e65f)
- Wikipedia infobox: opened September 17, 1837 (preliminary trip) and September 28, 1837 (regular service); rebuilt February 1, 1916; electrified September 22, 1930 — [Wikipedia: South Orange station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange_station)
- HPC timeline: in 1914 the DL&W "begins re-building the tracks through town, raising them above street level". In 1930 "a frail Thomas Edison inaugurates electric train service" between Hoboken and South Orange. Midtown Direct launched June 10, 1996. The station centennial was celebrated in 2016; "Built in 1916 by architect Frank J. Nies" — [HPC South Orange Historic Timeline (2014, with later entries)](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- NJ Register date and ID: not confirmed (see Gaps). The 2016 ordinance prints "ID# 4121" on the station's line, but the ID column in that PDF is visibly misaligned (see Section 2) — [2016 Ordinance, Schedule A](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2016-09-Ordinance-Establishing-a-Historic-Preservation-Commission-February-19-2016.pdf)

**1d. Mountain Station (NJ Transit), 449 Vose Avenue**
- NR 84002656, listed 9/29/1984 (Operating Passenger Railroad Stations TR). Architect "Nies, Frank J."; style Renaissance; significant year 1915 — [NPGallery NRIS 84002656](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/84002656)
- Survey form:
  - "Architect F. J. Nies or his staff; Date 1915; Style Renaissance Revival"
  - Trains per day: 69 in 1980; 96 in 1940; 74 in 1901 at the original station on site
  - A Mountain House-era landscape: "tile panel above the water fountain in the main waiting room which depicts a forest scene with stream, hills, and waterfall ... to recall a Tiffany-like art glass window"

- Former name "Montrose"; rebuilt 1870s and 1914–1915. It was the only point where the DL&W did not change the roadbed elevation, so Montrose Avenue was raised onto a bridge instead — [Wikipedia: Mountain Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Station)
- Date conflict: Montrose HD nomination: "449 Vose Avenue, the Mountain Railroad Station, was built c. 1910" — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). Contradicted by the 1915 date in the station's own survey and NR record — [NPGallery 84002656](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/84002656)
- Mountain Station is also a key building inside the Montrose Park HD — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)

**1e. Stone House by the Stone House Brook ("Old Stone House")**
- NR 87001333, listed 11/22/1991. NR address "219 S. Orange Ave."; architects "Unknown"; styles Queen Anne and Shingle Style; significant name "Brewer, William Augustus, Jr."; significant years 1773, 1966; criteria Person and Information Potential — [NPGallery NRIS 87001333](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/87001333)
- NJ Register October 4, 1991, NJRHP #1364 — [Wikipedia: Stone House by the Stone House Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_House_by_the_Stone_House_Brook)
- Address conflict:
  - The NR record and the nomination amendment say "Formerly 219 South Orange Avenue; near the corner of Grove Road and South Orange Avenue" — [NR nomination, Stone House (NPGallery PDF)](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
  - The Montrose HD nomination inventory lists it as "209 South Orange Avenue, Block 1007 Lot 13" — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - The Village's 2016 ordinance says "201 South Orange Avenue (previously known as 219 South Orange Ave)", Block/Lot 1007/13 — [2016 Ordinance, Schedule A](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2016-09-Ordinance-Establishing-a-Historic-Preservation-Commission-February-19-2016.pdf)
  - SOHPS gives only "behind the South Orange police station, between Grove Road and South Orange Avenue" — [SOHPS: Old Stone House](https://sohps.org/?p=7)
- Construction date conflict:
  - NR nomination: "The oldest part of the house was in existence before 1680 (Minutes of the Newark Town Meeting of September 27, 1680)". Period of significance "c. 1747-1916" — [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
  - Montrose HD nomination: "dating to c. 1670" — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - SOHPS: built "between 1666 and 1680" — [SOHPS](https://sohps.org/?p=7)
  - HPC timeline: "1680 ... The Stone House is built on South Orange Avenue and Grove Road" — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- Building phases, per the NR nomination:
  - Original 1½-story "Dutch Colonial rural tradition" rubble-stone house
  - "Hall and parlor stone addition built 1773-1791" by Bethuel Pierson
  - Queen Anne-Shingle Style addition in 1877 and again before 1896
  - Rear office addition in 1937 and after, by Dr. George Albee (Village building permit June 11, 1937)
  - Three original stone walls are visible outside and a fourth inside
  - The nomination calls it "the first dwelling to have been built in what is now South Orange"
  - [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Chain of title, per the NR nomination:
  - Nathaniel Wheeler acquired the land in 1680. Wheeler, Edward Riggs and Joseph Riggs had a grant "near the Stone House".
  - Samuel Pierson; Dr. Bethuel Pierson from 1773; Nathaniel Condit, who purchased in 1793
  - Daniel Lindsley, October 26, 1804; Moses Lindsley; Jeptha B. Lindsley, June 1, 1847
  - Alexander Bell, November 19, 1850; Philip Kingsley, October 4, 1851
  - Sheriff's sale to Ebenezer Deas. The text gives both "October 2, 1842" and "October 2, 1852"; the 1852 date matches Liber D8 p.154.
  - John Gorham Vose and Myra R. Vose, September 20, 1866
  - William A. Brewer Jr., 1866 (moved in 1867). He named the house "Aldworth".
  - Sold to George C. and Mary R. Albee on January 12, 1916, after Bella C. Brewer's death on September 15, 1915
  - Village of South Orange, July 1, 1953 (Liber 3147, p.106)
  - [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Date conflict on the Albee sale: Wikipedia says "In 1915, the house was sold to Dr. George C. Albee" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_House_by_the_Stone_House_Brook). Contradicted by the nomination's deed date of January 12, 1916 — [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Brewer served as Village President in 1877-78 and 1879-80. His improvements included "road, bridge, and sidewalk building, the illumination by gas lights, erection of street signs, public sewage". He was VP (1869) and then President (1879-1904) of Washington Life Insurance Co. of New York — [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Later use: part of the land was taken for the police station. The house was headquarters of the South Orange-Maplewood Board of Education until 1983 and vacant afterward. Phase I/II archaeology by Richard Grubb & Associates in June 1990 found two archaeological sites (a trash scatter and a midden). The nomination was prepared by Dr. David L. Gassman, revised January 2, 1991 (original application December 12, 1986) — [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Preservation events:
  - NJ Historic Trust grants in 2001 and 2002 for stabilization — [SOHPS](https://sohps.org/?p=7)
  - 1991: much of the remaining land sold for $1 to the South Mountain B'nai B'rith Federation for senior housing; 2008: stabilization work; 2013: an unsuccessful attempt to sell — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_House_by_the_Stone_House_Brook)
  - Preservation New Jersey's 10 Most Endangered Historic Places, May 2020 — [SOHPS](https://sohps.org/?p=7)
  - Current owner: Township of South Orange Village. SOHPS is raising funds; current use is vacant — [SOHPS](https://sohps.org/?p=7)

**1g. South Orange Fire Department (Fire Headquarters)**
- NR 98000255. Location "Jct. of First and Sloan Sts." (city recorded as "South Orange Village"). Architects "Dillon, Arthur; Hinman, Russell"; significant year 1925 — [NPGallery NRIS 98000255](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/98000255)
- NR listing date conflict: the NPGallery "Date Published" and the Wikipedia Essex list give 3/19/1998 (1998-03-19) — [NPGallery](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/98000255); [Wikipedia Essex list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Essex_County,_New_Jersey). Contradicted by the Wikipedia article infobox, which gives June 22, 1998 — [Wikipedia: South Orange Fire House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange_Fire_House)
- NJ Register January 28, 1998, NJRHP #41 — [Wikipedia: South Orange Fire House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange_Fire_House)
- Nomination details:
  - Three-story, Norman-influenced ("French Eclectic, or Norman Revival") masonry firehouse, built on a 45-degree diagonal to Sloan and First Streets
  - Two octagonal towers with "candle-snuffer" roofs; the western tower, about five stories, holds hose drying and the air siren
  - Trustees' ordinance of January 9, 1925; $70,000 in bonds
  - Architect Arthur Dillon of Dillon and Beadel, a South Orange resident trained at the École des Beaux-Arts; contractor Russell Hinman
  - "Construction began in 1925, and the firehouse was completed by December of 1926"
  - Integrity paragraph: "essentially the same as it was built in 1927"; only the overhead doors have been replaced
  - [NR nomination, Fire Dept (NPGallery PDF)](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b)
- Department history in the nomination:
  - Volunteer department organized February 2, 1891, first housed in the railroad roundhouse at the foot of Lackawanna Place
  - Moved into Village Hall in 1894
  - First paid chief, William Ash, appointed June 1, 1913
  - First motorized hook and ladder in 1914
  - An auxiliary station on Crest Drive opened in the late 1960s
  - [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b)
  - Conflict: SOPL says the 1891 department was "housed in a stable on Vose Avenue" — [SOPL wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History)
- Move-in date conflict: the HPC timeline ("1930 NEW FIREHOUSE"), the SOPL wiki and the Village history page all say the department moved from Village Hall in 1930 — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline); [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History). This conflicts with the nomination's completion date of December 1926 — [NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b)
- The building closed April 22, 2002 because of asbestos and was renovated and reopened "several years later" (no date given). Wikipedia calls it headquarters of the "South Essex Fire Department" and credits the architects as "Arthur Dillon and Henry L Beadel" — [Wikipedia: South Orange Fire House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange_Fire_House)

### Inferences
- The NJ HPO's sequential IDs 1360–1364 appear to have been assigned alphabetically to South Orange's older listings. Kelly = 1360, Village Hall = 1363 and Stone House = 1364 are confirmed by Wikipedia. That pattern implies Mountain Station = 1361 and South Orange Station = 1362. This is not confirmed by a primary source; verify against the NJ HPO Essex list before publishing.
- The station NR listings (1984) also put both stations on the NJ Register under the Operating Passenger Railroad Stations Thematic Resource. Wikipedia's Mountain Station article says it "has been listed in the New Jersey Register ... since 1984", but no exact SR date was found.

### Gaps
- The NJ HPO "New Jersey and National Registers – Essex County" list (the authoritative source for SR dates, SHPO Opinion dates and IDs) could not be retrieved: nj.gov returned 403/404 and archive.org was blocked. SR dates/IDs for South Orange Station, Mountain Station and the Old Main DL&W Railroad HD are unconfirmed.
- The Village Hall NR nomination is a scan with partial OCR, so some text was illegible.
- No exact street number was found for the Kelly Carriage House (only "South Orange Ave., Seton Hall campus") or for the firehouse (only "First and Sloan Sts.").
- The firehouse reopening date after the 2002 asbestos closure was not found.

## 2. Local landmarks and local historic districts (South Orange Historic Preservation Commission)

### Takeaway
South Orange's local designations are set out in Schedule A ("Local Landmarks") of the March 14, 2016 HPC ordinance. Schedule A lists:
- the Montrose Park Historic District with 40 named "key" properties,
- the Old Main DL&W Railroad HD and the Prospect Street HD, neither with designated key properties,
- eight "Other Historic Properties": the Chapel of the Immaculate Conception, the Kelly Carriage House, Mountain Station, the Fire House, South Orange Station, Village Hall, the Old Stone House and the former Riker Mansion / Temple Sharey Tefilo-Israel.

As of October 2024 the only designated residential properties were the 40 Montrose key contributing properties. No separate per-property designation reports were found online. The designation basis is the State/National Register status listed in Schedule A.

### Cited Findings

**Ordinance history**

## 3. Montrose Park Historic District: the 40 locally designated "key" properties

### Takeaway
The 40 key properties named in the 2016 Schedule A match the "Key" buildings in the 1997 NR nomination inventory. Most are estate houses built c. 1866–1925 with documented early owners. Several are institutional: the Connett Library, Mountain Station, the Old Stone House, Temple Sharey Tefilo-Israel, the Marylawn/Graves house and a Seton Hall building. The nomination's addresses differ in three places from Schedule A.

### Cited Findings
All entries below are from the 1997 [Montrose Park HD NR nomination inventory](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). ## 4. Other notable buildings and places (non-listed or partly listed)

### Takeaway
Beyond the register listings, South Orange's notable historic places include:
- Seton Hall's Presidents Hall (1867) and Chapel of the Immaculate Conception (cornerstone 1863, dedicated 1870), both by Jeremiah O'Rourke. These come from search snippets only.
- The Connett Library (opened May 8, 1896).
- The Baird Center (South Orange Field Club, renamed 1930).
- Cameron Field, the 1937 Post Office with its Bernard Perlin mural, Memorial Park (the village cemetery, 1828–1926), the Memorial Flagpole (c. 1924), Memorial Rock and Tony Smith's Tau.
- Grove Park (1908), Meadowland Park with the Duck Pond and Flood's Hill, and about 1,400 gas lamps.
- Orange Lawn Tennis Club (in the Redmond "Hillside" mansion since 1916), the Squier House (c. 1774), First Presbyterian & Trinity Church (1880 brownstone) and Our Lady of Sorrows (1931).

"Old Stone House": the NR-listed Old Stone House is genuinely in South Orange. No reliable source for "Peabody" houses in South Orange was found, and the Ball houses are Maplewood's.

### Cited Findings

**Seton Hall University (campus address used by the Village for the Chapel: 400 South Orange Avenue)**
- April 1860: Bishop James Roosevelt Bayley bought about 60 acres on both sides of South Orange Avenue from the Elphinstone family, including "a marble villa, farmhouse, and stables". The first college building opened to students in September 1860. The north-side farmland was a dairy farm, sold off in 1922 (15 acres at SOA and Centre Street) and fully developed by 1928 — [Montrose HD nomination, Sec. 8](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Chapel of the Immaculate Conception [search-snippet only]:
  - Cornerstone laid May 21, 1863 by Bishop Bayley; dedicated 1870
  - A dedication account says it is "built of stone, was designed by Mr. J. O'Rourke, architect, built by O'Rourke & Moran, and decorated by J. R. Lamb, of New York"
  - Around 1906 an arched passageway was built linking it to the Seminary (Presidents Hall) and the bell tower was removed
  - [Seton Hall Chapel blog: Overview](https://blogs.shu.edu/chapel/?p=127); [Chapel blog: Early Years](https://blogs.shu.edu/chapel/?p=37)
- Presidents Hall [search-snippet only]: dates to 1867; originally the Seminary Building of the Immaculate Conception Seminary; designed by Newark architect Jeremiah O'Rourke — [Seton Hall Chapel blog](https://blogs.shu.edu/chapel/?p=127); [Seton Hall catalogue: university buildings](https://catalogue.shu.edu/undergraduate/university-buildings)
- The Kelly Carriage House is NR-listed (Section 1a). The Kelly mansion itself was demolished — [Kelly NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6b724602-5940-4970-807f-05c2e049c9a5)

**Churches**
- First Presbyterian & Trinity Church:
  - Brownstone church "built in 1880", replacing an older church on the same triangular lot (South Orange and Irvington Avenues)
  - 1929 Gothic Revival Education Building facing South Orange Avenue. It looks like limestone but is cast cement; its facade was restored by Approved Construction (Louis DeMary) around late 2025
  - SOHPS gave the congregation an award in December 2025. Rose windows by Henry Keck. The South Orange Symphony was founded there
  - [Essex Review, Darren Tobia, Dec. 29, 2025](https://essexreview.com/firstpresbyteriansouthorange)
  - A church directory gives "111 Irvington Avenue", says the oldest part was built 1881 with worship since 1882, and says First Presbyterian (organized 1831) and Trinity (organized 1892) merged in 1935. This is a low-reliability aggregator [search-snippet only] — [learnofchrist.com directory](https://learnofchrist.com/churches/new-jersey/south-orange/first-presbyterian-and-trinity-church-c1f33547)
  - Date conflict: 1880 (Essex Review) vs 1881 (directory)
  - The 1834 Gordon's Gazetteer counts "a paper mill and Presbyterian church" in South Orange village — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - In 1894 the Presbyterian Church owned a lot at Prospect and Irvington Avenues that was proposed for Village Hall — [SOPL wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History)

  - [Our Lady of Sorrows School: School History](https://www.ourladyofsorrowsschool.org/school-history)

A directory lists "The Episcopal Church of St. Andrew and Holy Communion" at "160 South Orange Avenue", which is unverified [search-snippet only] — [devout.org directory](https://www.devout.org/churches/nj/south-orange/the-episcopal-church-of-st-andrew-and-holy-communion)

**Library buildings**
- Connett (Memorial) Library, Scotland Road and Taylor Place:
  - The South Orange Library Association was founded in November 1864 by William Beebe. From 1864 to 1889 it occupied upper floors on Sloan Street; one building was physically moved up the Avenue to Scotland Road and South Orange Avenue (1884 per the HPC timeline). It became a free library in 1886.
  - In 1895 Eugene V. Connett offered the lot, conditional on $7,500 being raised. Ground was broken September 23, 1895, and the plans were by "Messrs. Stephenson & Greene" of New York. The building is Indiana limestone and light grey pressed brick with a red slate roof, 54 x 30 ft. Books moved in in April and the formal opening was May 8, 1896.
  - It was renamed for the Connetts after Mrs. Connett's death in 1912. A rear wing with a children's room opened in 1930.
  - The new library was dedicated in November 1968. The old building later housed an organization serving the blind.
  - [SOPL wiki: Connett (Thomas Vilardi)](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Connett)
  - HPC timeline: 1929 Trustees' ordinance funding the rear wing; 1968 new library at "Scotland Road and Comstock Place" — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- Architect conflict: Stephenson & Greene, per the 1896 annual report quoted on [SOPL wiki](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Connett). Contradicted by the Fire Dept NR nomination, which says Arthur Dillon "was also responsible for the design of Connett Memorial Library on Scotland Road" — [Fire Dept NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b). Dillon may have designed the 1929–30 wing, but that is unverified.
- Address conflict: "55 Scotland Road" (Block 1005/4) in [2016 Schedule A](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2016-09-Ordinance-Establishing-a-Historic-Preservation-Commission-February-19-2016.pdf) vs "59 Scotland Road" (same Block/Lot) in the [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The current library building's street address was not found in these sources.

**Baird Center / South Orange Field Club / Meadowland Park / Cameron Field**
- In 1889 residents formed the Meadow Land Society and bought 23 acres from Edwin H. Mead to keep industry out of the Rahway River valley; William F. Allen, "the inventor of Standard Time", was president. The South Orange Field Club formed the same year, and Mead's barn served as clubhouse.
- The barn burned "in January of 1895" and a new building was erected immediately. "The South Orange Field Club is now Meadowlands Park and the clubhouse is now the Baird Community Center" — [Montrose HD nomination, Sec. 8](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Fire date conflict: the SOPL wiki says the barn "burned to the ground" in January 1896. It also says that in 1930, 14 rooms were added and the club was renamed the Baird Center — [SOPL wiki: Baird Community Center](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Baird_Community_Center)
- Cameron Field:
  - Owned by the South Orange Field Club from about 1900 to the mid-1920s, with a 9-hole "Lone Oak Links" golf course; Negro League teams played there — [SOPL wiki: Cameron Field](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Cameron_Field)
  - "On Oct. 27, 1929, Babe Ruth and Lou Gehrig played baseball at Cameron Field"; its centennial was celebrated in 2014 — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- The Duck Pond is the early-20th-century "Meadowland Park Lake". The Rahway River ground was marshy "prior to drainage in 1881-1883" [search-snippet only; HMDB blocked direct fetch] — [HMDB marker 236491](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=236491); [HMDB marker 238297](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=238297)
- The park is described as about 45 acres in a Meadowland Park Conservancy event story [search-snippet only] — [Village Green](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=108122)
- Flood's Hill: Henry Squier built 167 North Ridgewood Road in 1774. William Redmond acquired it in 1850 and leased it to a dairy farmer named Flood, who "pastures his cows in Meadowbrook [sic] Park". Flood's Hill, "one of the best snow sledding spots in Essex County, is named after his family" — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline); [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- Memorial Rock (by the Duck Pond) has three plaques for 70 South Orange dead of World War II (59), Korea (6) and Vietnam (5) — [SOPL wiki: Memorial Rock](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Memorial_Rock)
- Tau by South Orange native Tony Smith: black-painted steel, 14 x 21 x 12 ft, modeled 1961–62. It stands on the slope between the Duck Pond and North Ridgewood Road.
  - Installation date conflict: "installed in 2009" per [SOPL wiki: Tau](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Tau); "2008" per the [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
  - Smith lived and worked on Stanley Road in Montrose Park (house number not given) — [SOPL wiki: Tau](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Tau)
- "Waterlands": no source found under this name.

**Grove Park**
- The land was bought in 1908 by citizens, who raised $8,000 and transferred it to the Village Trustees; another $2,000 paid for landscaping. It was the only park within Village limits at the time — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Size and boundary conflict, within the same nomination:
  - The inventory says "8.09 acre park, bounded by Grove Road, South Orange Avenue, Park Place, and Raymond Avenue"; the photo caption gives "351 South Orange Avenue"
  - The narrative says "a five acre tract of land bounded by South Orange Avenue, Grove Road and Strathern Avenue"
- Grove Park "originally formed part of the farm connected to" the Old Stone House — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)

**Memorials and civic buildings**
- Memorial Park (South Orange; not Maplewood Memorial Park): site of the Village Cemetery from 1828 to 1926. The cemetery was vandalized, and in 1926 a 10-ft monument listing those buried there replaced it. The location/address is not given in the source — [SOPL wiki: Memorial Park](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Memorial_Park)
- Memorial Flagpole, on an island at South Orange Avenue and Irvington Avenue: bronze on granite, "constructed circa 1924", cost $20,000; WWI names (6 South Orange dead) — [SOPL wiki: Memorial Flagpole](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Memorial_Flagpole)
- South Orange War Monument "on the Village Green" / "old village common", by Arthur Dillon — [Fire Dept NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b). It may be the same object as the Memorial Flagpole; unverified.
- Post Office (Vose Avenue): the present building opened in 1937. Bernard Perlin, then 21, won the $2,000 commission for its mural in the 1939 Treasury competition — [SOPL wiki: South Orange Post Office](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/South_Orange_Post_Office); "1937: present post office opened on Vose Avenue" — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- The first U.S. post office opened in 1841 in Freeman's Store, 71 South Orange Avenue — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- Police headquarters: in March 1972 a separate police station and Municipal Court building on South Orange Avenue (near Grove Road, on former Stone House land) was completed — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History); [SOPL wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History)
- SOPAC (South Orange Performing Arts Center): "2006 ... opens" — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline). Its address, architect and building history were not researched.

**Gas lamps**
- Center-of-town street lamps burned sperm oil until 1860, when gas service arrived. Electric power came around 1888, "but most of the streets are still lit by gas lamps" — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History); also [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- Gas street lighting was among W.A. Brewer Jr.'s improvements as Village President, 1877–80 — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Count conflict: "1,400 iconic, historic gas lamps" — [Village Green](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=43236) [search-snippet only]; "nearly 1,500" — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/update-turning-off-the-gas-lights) [search-snippet only]. PSE&G owns the lamps, per search summaries only.
- The gaslight and Village Hall are "the symbol of South Orange" — [HPC letter, 2014 (quoting Wikipedia)](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/HPC-Ltr-to-Trustees.pdf). The Montrose nomination notes "Victorian gaslight", bluestone sidewalks and Belgian block gutters — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)

**Orange Lawn Tennis Club (305 North Ridgewood Road)**
- Address 305 North Ridgewood Road, South Orange. Founded 1880 at Montrose and Berkeley Avenues, where it stayed 36 years.
- Club timeline: 1881, one of 19 clubs that standardized the rules; 1885–1920, hosted the Middle States Championships; 1886, hosted the national doubles championships; 1916, moved to "Hillside," part of the Redmond Estate; 2018, new ownership; 2019–2020, clubhouse interior renovation preserving the mural.
- Later events: Althea Gibson in 1956, Billie Jean Moffitt (King) in 1964, Chris Evert in 1971, John McEnroe in 1979.
- [Orange Lawn Tennis Club: Our Story](https://www.orangelawn.com/our-story)
- Founding details: founded October 4, 1880, first president Richard J. Cross, 10-acre site; a founding member of the USTA in May 1881; bought in 2018 by investors led by Bruce Schonbraun — [Wikipedia: Orange Lawn Tennis Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Lawn_Tennis_Club)
- Championship year conflict: the club site and the Montrose nomination say the national championships were hosted in 1886. Wikipedia says 1887 (men's doubles) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Lawn_Tennis_Club)
- Original site: 10 acres leased "between Montrose and Warwick Avenues, east of Berkeley Avenue", part of the John G. Vose estate, with 8 grass and 2 clay courts. In 1916 the officers bought the Redmond estate "Hillside" on Ridgewood Road. Members who stayed behind formed the Berkeley Tennis Club in the City of Orange — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The clubhouse is the brownstone mansion William Redmond built as his home — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History). The HPC timeline puts it in "1830 ... William Redmond builds the home that is now used as the Orange Lawn Tennis Club" — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- Postcard conflict: a Seton Hall postcard is titled "The Old Redmond House, Built in 1774 Ridgewood Road, South Orange, NJ" — [SHU eRepository postcard 115](https://scholarship.shu.edu/so-postcards/115). The 1774 date matches the Squier House (167 N. Ridgewood Rd, acquired by Redmond in 1850), not the c.1830 brownstone clubhouse, so the postcard probably shows the Squier House.

**Historic houses outside Montrose**
- Squier House, 167 North Ridgewood Road: built c. 1774 by Henry Squier (later a Revolutionary War lieutenant); acquired by William Redmond in 1850 and leased to dairyman Flood — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
  - Plans submitted in November 2020 would have demolished it. Architect Bill MacIntosh proposed a three-lot alternative to save it — [Village Green: "A New Plan Could Save South Orange's Historic Squier House"](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=78507); [Village Green: architect to present plans to Trustees Feb. 22](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=78875)
  - SOHPS reports "two first floor rooms including the stone work" were incorporated into a new house; the 1920s second floor was removed [search-snippet only] — [SOHPS](https://www.sohps.org)
  - The Township had no power to block demolition because the house was not designated — [Village Green, Feb. 15, 2024](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=99410)
  - Tradition says Nathan Squier first used the name "South Orange" in print, in a c. 1793 Wood's Gazette ad — [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- Another stone house: "Only one other stone house still survives in South Orange, and this has been moved some distance" (unidentified) — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- Marylawn / Graves mansion, 425 Scotland Road: c. 1900 Richardsonian Romanesque home of Edward H. Graves, a NYC finance businessman. Marylawn of the Oranges bought it in 1935. In 1956 it removed another historic house at Scotland Road and Montrose Avenue to build its school.
  - In May 2012 the Sisters of Christian Charity sought a demolition permit to sell the 3-acre site for apartments, opposed by the Montrose Park Historic District Association — [Patch, Marcia Worth, May 9, 2012](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/montrose-mansion-may-face-demolition)
  - The school's closure for June 2013 was announced in October 2012. Since March 2018 the site has housed the STEM Innovation Academy [search-snippet only] — [Wikipedia: Marylawn of the Oranges Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylawn_of_the_Oranges_Academy)
  - The mansion remains a designated key property in 2016 Schedule A — [2016 Ordinance](https://villagegreennj.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2016-09-Ordinance-Establishing-a-Historic-Preservation-Commission-February-19-2016.pdf)

**Maplewood/South Orange disambiguation**
- South Orange Township was created in 1861. On May 4, 1869, South Orange Village separated from "the Township of South Orange (now Maplewood)", and full autonomy came only in 1904. Pre-1904 references to "South Orange Township" may therefore mean Maplewood territory — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3); [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
- The Village annexed land from the Montrose section in 1891, and in 1925 added land on the side of First Watchung Mountain north of South Orange Avenue — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)

### Inferences
- The task's suspicion about the "Old Stone House" should be resolved this way. South Orange's Old Stone House (the NR-listed Stone House by the Stone House Brook) is real and belongs on the South Orange list. Any similarly named Maplewood building must be kept separate; no Maplewood "Old Stone House" was checked in this research.
- "Memorial Park" exists in both towns. South Orange's is the former Village Cemetery (1928 monument site, unlisted). Maplewood Memorial Park is the NR-listed one. Landmark pages must not merge them.
- "Peabody/Ball houses": the Timothy Ball House is Maplewood. Treat any "Ball house" as Maplewood unless a South Orange-specific source appears.

- "Waterlands" could not be identified.

## 5. Lost landmarks (demolished or relocated)

### Takeaway
The major documented losses are:
- the Mountain House water-cure hotel (c. 1830), burned 1890 per the NR nominations (1881 per Village sources)
- the Kelly mansion (Seton Hall)
- the Elphinstone marble villa (Seton Hall's first residence), whose fate is undocumented
- Orange Lawn's original 1880–1916 Montrose grounds
- the ground-level railroad stations before the 1914–16 elevation, and the railroad roundhouse
- the Field Club's Mead barn
- the Village Cemetery (1828–1926)
- the George W. Maher-designed Arthur Burtis Leach House at 321 Scotland Road
- the Squier House, largely rebuilt c. 2021

### Cited Findings
- **Mountain House (Orange Springs / water-cure hotel and spa):**
  - Built c. 1830 on the west side of Ridgewood Road "at the foot of the present Glenside Road". A large wooden hotel with two wings for 150 guests, supervised by two physicians, with spring water piped down the mountain.
  - The Eclipse Stage Line ran to Newark in 1830. In 1850 it was owned by "Mr. Lord of Lord & Taylor" and leased to G. Baird.
  - Only Mountain Station and Mountain House Road remain — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History); [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - Fire date conflict: "burnt down on August 23,1890" per the [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd) and "August 23, 1890" per the [Fire Dept NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b). Contradicted by "1881 ... destroyed by fire" in the [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline) and "Destroyed by fire after 1881" on the [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History) page.
  - The Fire Dept nomination ties the 1890 fire to the founding of the volunteer fire department (February 2, 1891) — [Fire Dept NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b)
- **Kelly mansion, Seton Hall:** Eugene Kelly enlarged it in 1887 in the Queen Anne style; "now demolished" by 1975 (demolition date not given) — [Kelly NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6b724602-5940-4970-807f-05c2e049c9a5)
- **Elphinstone marble villa:** Seton Hall bought it in 1860 and used it as a faculty and student residence — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). Its later fate was not found.
- **Original Orange Lawn Tennis Club grounds (1880–1916):** 10 leased acres between Montrose and Warwick Avenues, east of Berkeley Avenue (Vose estate), with 8 grass and 2 clay courts. The club left in 1916 — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd); [OLTC: Our Story](https://www.orangelawn.com/our-story). The site is presumably now houses; not verified.
- **19th-century railroad stations:**
  - The Morris & Essex reached South Orange in 1836–37 and was extended to Hoboken in 1868 — [Village: History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
  - An "Original station on site" was in service in 1901 at both South Orange and Mountain — [SO Station survey](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/9a4e511d-181e-4493-80cd-21d265a8e65f); [Mountain Station survey](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/5a4d691c-88b5-47ff-8127-e451775926df)
  - The first station was at ground level. Track elevation began in 1914, with yearlong construction — [SOPL wiki: The Train Station](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/The_Train_Station)
  - A railroad roundhouse stood at the foot of Lackawanna Place and was the fire department's first home in 1891 — [Fire Dept NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/1c0a8af1-a768-4ade-9632-e0c9fdb7606b)
  - The Steamtown NHS archives have photos of the 1914 elevation project — [Village: Historic Resources](https://southorange.org/1068/Historic-Resources)
- **Edwin H. Mead's barn (first Field Club clubhouse):** burned January 1895 per the [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd), or January 1896 per the [SOPL wiki](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Baird_Community_Center).
- **Village Cemetery (1828–1926):** replaced by Memorial Park and its monument in 1926 — [SOPL wiki: Memorial Park](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Memorial_Park)
- **Arthur Burtis Leach House ("Pleasant Home"), 321 Scotland Road:** by George W. Maher, 1899; listed as demolished (no date) — [Wikipedia: Montrose Park HD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montrose_Park_Historic_District)
- **Other losses in Montrose:**
  - 37 Kingman Road had been demolished by 1997, leaving only its garage — [Montrose HD nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - A historic house at Scotland Road and Montrose Avenue was removed in 1956 for the Marylawn school building — [Patch, May 9, 2012](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/montrose-mansion-may-face-demolition)
  - Brewer's "substantial building" south of the Stone House stood on the present police station site — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3)
- **Early library and municipal quarters:**
  - The 1864–1889 library rooms on Sloan Street included Beck's Hardware. One building was moved up the Avenue in 1884 to Scotland Road and South Orange Avenue — [SOPL wiki: Connett](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Connett); [HPC Timeline](https://southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/17290/HPC_South-Orange-Timeline)
  - Before 1895 the Trustees met in the Smith and Lum building, then the Becker Building at "#6 and #8 South Orange Avenue", which was "still standing" c. 1975 — [SOPL wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History)
  - The "stately Columbia School building" on South Orange Avenue stood opposite Village Hall in 1894 — [SOPL wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History). Brewer's era saw "a much larger Columbian school constructed" — [Stone House NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/d73c09bc-36a7-496d-9bdb-84704a0a48c3). Its fate was not found. Do not confuse it with Columbia High School in Maplewood.
- **Squier House (c. 1774), 167 N. Ridgewood Road:** mostly replaced; only the two oldest rooms were kept in a new house [search-snippet only] — [SOHPS](https://www.sohps.org); [Village Green](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=78507)
- **Village Hall belfry and bell:** the belfry was possibly removed around 1930 and the 1,000-lb bell "disappeared" — [SOPL wiki: Village Hall History](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Village_Hall_History). The 1975 nomination notes the original cupola and bell visible in a 1906 postcard — [Village Hall NR nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/7b7019c2-f7e2-43db-aec9-ce246907eaca). The 2021 restoration of the clock tower is mentioned in news summaries only — [Village Green](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=83445) [search-snippet only].

### Inferences
- The Mountain House fire-date conflict is most likely settled in favor of August 23, 1890. Two independent NR nominations give the exact date and link it to the fire department's founding in February 1891. Village sources that say 1881 may be confusing it with the 1881–83 drainage or with another fire. Check an 1890 South Orange Bulletin or NY Times before publishing.

### Gaps
- No demolition dates were found for the Kelly mansion, the Elphinstone villa, the Columbia/Columbian School or the Leach House.
- The South Orange station building that preceded the 1916 station (architect, date) was not documented beyond its 1901 existence.
- The "Peabody" estate and other lost Gilded Age estates (e.g., Connett, Vose, Kingman residences) were not researched individually.
- HMDB markers for South Orange (Meadowland Park Lake, Flood's Hill, Lone Oak golf course, William Redmond "Hillside") exist but could not be read (403). Their verbatim inscriptions should be retrieved for the landmark pages: [HMDB 236491](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=236491), [238297](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=238297), [236494](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=236494), [256373](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=256373), [238293](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=238293).
