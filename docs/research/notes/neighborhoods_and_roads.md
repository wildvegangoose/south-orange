# South Orange, NJ: Historic Neighborhoods, Their Development, and Historic Roads

Scope note: South Orange only (Township of South Orange Village, Essex County). Maplewood material appears only where it explains a shared road, such as Valley Street or Ridgewood Road. Maplewood also has a "Wyoming" neighborhood, and the Township of Millburn has a "Wyoming" section. The South Orange "Lower Wyoming" and "Upper Wyoming" neighborhoods are separate places.

## 1. Which named neighborhoods/sections actually exist in South Orange (verified names)?

### Takeaway
The Village's own 2021 Master Plan names ten residential neighborhoods: Montrose Park, West Montrose, Academy Heights, Seton Village, Tuxedo Park, Village Colonials, South Mountain, Lower Wyoming, Upper Wyoming, and Newstead. The downtown is the Central Business District. Only Montrose Park is a designated (NR/SR) historic district. Prospect Street has a SHPO opinion of eligibility. South Ridgewood Road, North Ridgewood Road, Riggs Place, Meeker Street, Hillside Place, and Tuxedo Park are mapped as potentially eligible districts. "Flood's Hill," "Cameron Field," and "Grove Park" are parks, not neighborhoods. "Murray Hill" does not appear in any South Orange planning source.

### Cited Findings
- The Master Plan steering committee had "Neighborhood Ambassador Chairs" for Montrose Park, Tuxedo Park, Seton Village, South Mountain ("South Mountian" [sic]), West Montrose, Lower Wyoming, Upper Wyoming, and Newstead. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The Master Plan's lot-size analysis adds "Academy Heights" and "Village Colonials" as neighborhoods. Quote: "The majority of lots in West Montrose, Academy Heights, Seton Village, Tuxedo Park, Village Colonials, South Mountain, the southern portion of Montrose Park and the northernmost part of Lower and Upper Wyoming are between 5,000 and 10,000 square feet." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The Master Plan treats Holland Road as a street "in Montrose Park that abuts the municipal boundary with Newark," zoned RA-50, with many lots under 5,000 sq ft. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The 2007 Historic Districts map lists one National and State Register district (Montrose Park Historic District) and one "SHPO Opinion" district (Prospect Street Historic District). It lists six "Potential Eligible National Register Districts": South Ridgewood Road, North Ridgewood Road, Riggs Place, Meeker Street, Hillside Place, and Tuxedo Park. — [HD-Map](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/246/Historic-Districts-PDF-)
- The Master Plan describes the historic resources in sub-areas: "Academy Heights/Prospect Street Historic District"; "Seton Village from Cottage Street to Ward Place"; "South Mountain – South Ridgewood Road"; "West Montrose" (proposed Meeker Street and Hillside Place HDs); "Montrose Park"; "North Ridgewood Road + Lower Wyoming" (proposed North Ridgewood Road HD); "Lower Wyoming South"; "Upper Wyoming"; "South Mountain – South Ridgewood" (20th c.); "Newstead"; "Tuxedo Park"; and "Central Business District." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The 1986 Essex County Cultural Resources Survey recommended surveys for possible Meeker Street, Hillside Place, and North Ridgewood Road historic districts. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- As of October 2024, the only designated residential local landmarks were "the 40 properties identified as 'key contributing properties' in the Montrose Park Historic District." — [HPC FAQ, 2024](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/13509). The HPC has invited owners of contributing Montrose properties to self-nominate under Ordinance #2024-16, adopted Sept 9, 2024. — [HPC letter](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/13508)
- Parks named in the Master Plan include Meadowland Park, Cameron Field, and Floods Hill. Wikipedia lists Grove Park, New Waterlands Park, Flood's Hill, Cameron Field, and Farrell Field. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey)
- Historic sub-name: in the 19th century "Montrose" was treated locally as a separate settlement. T.P. Bayes' Orange Directory listed Montrose separately from South Orange from 1871 until 1878. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- In the 1930s Tuxedo Park residents often used "Tuxedo Park" in mailing addresses, and NYT social notes sometimes dropped "South Orange." — [Patch, "Tuxedo Park Marks a Century"](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/tuxedo-park-marks-a-century)

### Inferences
- For house-story assignment, use the MP2021 neighborhood names. They are the Village's own, current usage. "Montrose Park" can mean either the NR district (most of the NE quadrant) or, historically, Kingman's 1891 tract east of Centre Street. The house story should say which is meant.
- The user's candidate names map onto these neighborhoods as follows:
  - "Holland Road area" is part of Montrose Park.
  - "Grove Park/Prospect area" splits in two. Grove Park is inside the Montrose Park HD. Prospect Street is in Academy Heights.
  - "Village center along South Orange Avenue" is the CBD.
  - "Seton Hall area" includes Seton Village and the 1920s Seton Hall farm tract inside Montrose Park.
  - "Flood's Hill" and "Cameron Field" are parks, not neighborhoods.
  - "Murray Hill" appears to be a mis-recollection. No South Orange source uses it.
- CAUTION on map labels: OpenStreetMap/Nominatim neighbourhood tags often disagree with MP2021. Nominatim labels Riggs Place and Cottage Street "Academy Heights," but MP2021 puts them in Seton Village. It labels Harding Drive, Mayhew Drive, Twin Oak Road, Walton Avenue, and West End Road "Newstead," but MP2021 puts these in Upper Wyoming or South Mountain. It labels Tillou Road, Beech Spring Road, and Melrose Place "Newstead North," but MP2021 puts them in Lower Wyoming. Do not use OSM neighbourhood polygons to assign neighborhoods. — [Nominatim results, e.g.](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Harding+Drive,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json)

### Gaps
- No official boundary polygons for the ten MP2021 neighborhoods were found as text. The Master Plan has a neighborhood map, but it is an image, and the labels could not be fully extracted.
- I could not determine where "Village Colonials" lies (it is zoned RA-50 and has narrow lots), or when and by whom it was built.
- No source found for a South Orange "Academy Heights" name origin. Academy Street and the Columbian School are a plausible origin (see Section 3), but this is unconfirmed.

## 2. Montrose Park (the Montrose Park Historic District): history, developers, plats, styles, streets

### Takeaway
The neighborhood grew from farms into a commuter-elite enclave in three waves.
- **John Gorham Vose and Henry A. Page, 1860s–1874.** Vose bought farms beginning with the ~70-acre H.M. Graham farm and opened streets west of Centre Street. His lands were surveyed on Lewis P. Taylor's May 1873 map.
- **Thomas Sewall Kingman's syndicate, 1891 on.** Kingman bought the Aaron Bishop Baldwin farm east of Centre Street c.1886 and named it "Montrose Park." The land was annexed to the Village on Feb 10, 1891, and laid out with lots of at least 100×200 ft.
- **The 1920s.** Seton Hall's north-side dairy farm was sold in 1922 and fully built out by 1928.

The styles are dominated by Colonial Revival, then Shingle Style, Tudor Revival, and Queen Anne. The district is lit by gas lamps.

### Location / center / boundaries
- NR boundary: "bounded by So. Orange Ave., Holland Rd., City of Orange; NJ Transit railroad." In practice: the railroad on the west, the Orange/South Orange line on the north, the Newark line (Holland Road) on the east, and South Orange Avenue on the south. Henderson Drive and post-WWII commercial properties on South Orange Avenue south of Stone House Brook were excluded. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The district contains 1,129 buildings plus one site (Grove Park): 708 primary and 421 secondary. Of the primary buildings, 40 are key, 511 contributing, and 157 non-contributing. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Representative geocodes (OSM Nominatim street points): Centre Street 40.7493, -74.2405; Montrose Avenue 40.7496, -74.2439; Hartford Road 40.7510, -74.2429; Raymond Avenue 40.7488, -74.2532; Kingman Road 40.7458, -74.2401; Holland Road 40.7480, -74.2367; Grove Park 40.7468, -74.2503; Mountain Station 40.7553, -74.2528; University Court 40.7461, -74.2473. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Centre+Street,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json)

### Cited Findings: pre-suburban history (farms and estates)
- In 1834 Gordon's Gazetteer described South Orange as "a village ... on the turnpike from Newark to Morristown ... about 30 dwellings, a tavern and store; a paper mill and Presbyterian church." Settlement was then concentrated along South Orange Avenue, Prospect Street, and Irvington Avenue. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The area was sparsely populated farmland until the mid-19th century. The 1860 (Hughes) map shows these landowners:
  - West of Centre Street: Benjamin E. Baldwin, Ebenezer Deas, Samuel P. Smith.
  - East of Centre Street: Aaron Bishop Baldwin, Thomas D. Kilburn, G.H. Wheeler.
  - West of Scotland Street: D.H. Condit, Ira T. Freeman, Dorothea Freeman, Caleb Smith, Job Tillou, H.M. Graham, Lydia Freeman.

  Only South Orange Avenue (then "the Newark Turnpike"), Scotland Road, Montrose Avenue, and Centre Street existed. Apart from the houses on the turnpike there were only five homes, including the Old Stone House and the Baldwin house at 311 Centre Street. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Oldest buildings: the Old Stone House (201 South Orange Ave), believed built c.1680. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021). The Abel Ward House (497 South Orange Ave) is c.1770. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Bishop Baldwin House (311 Centre St, "House with the White Chimneys"/"Self House"): its date conflicts across sources.
  - MP2021 puts the oldest portion at c.1760. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
  - The NR inventory says "believed to be constructed c. 1806." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - The SOPL timeline lists a "Jeptha Baldwin homestead (circa 1760)." — [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline)
- Seton Hall bought about 60 acres on both sides of South Orange Avenue from the Elphinstone family in April 1860. It built on the south side and kept the north side as dairy pasture. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). CONFLICT: MP2021 says Seton Hall "acquired a 60-acre tract on the eastern side of town in 1856." 1856 is the seminary's founding in Madison per the NR. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

### Cited Findings: development eras and developers
- **Vose era (1860s–1874).**
  - John Gorham Vose (1829–1874) was a New York lawyer who summered c.1860 in a cottage on Scotland Street, north of Tremont Avenue in Orange. His first purchase was the ~70-acre H.M. Graham farm at the NE corner of Scotland Street and Montrose Avenue.
  - With Henry A. Page he laid out Montrose Avenue between Scotland Street and Valley Road (now Clark Street). He later bought the Ebenezer Deas property (Old Stone House) and eventually held more than 175 acres, from Centre Street west to Valley Road.
  - Vose opened sections of Montrose, Warwick, Raymond, and Ralston Avenues, plus Randolph Place and Grove Road. Across the Orange line he opened Haxtun and Stirling Avenues. He built "a palatial home" at the summit of Montrose Avenue (about 460 Scotland Road, now the Henderson Drive subdivision) and a number of speculative houses.

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **Plat/map dates.** In May 1873 surveyor Lewis P. Taylor drew the "Map of South Orange & Montrose, Essex County, NJ," covering Vose's lands and the lands of Taylor and G.W. Comstock (the former Daniel H. Condit farm).
  - New streets on the map: Raymond, Ralston, Charlton, and Irving Avenues, and Grove Road.
  - Proposed streets: Claremont Street (now Vose Avenue), Strathern Avenue (now Park Place), Randolph Place (now Stewart Place), Comstock Place, and Taylor Place.
  - Vose lots are numbered 200–285.

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Street openings Vose petitioned for as Village Trustee (Dec 1870–June 1872): Mosswood Avenue (Feb 1871) and Grove Road as a public street (April 1871). With William A. Brewer and D.G. Sprague he petitioned for Ralston Avenue (Sept 1873). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- "Vose Restrictions": large minimum lots, and no public buildings except houses of worship. Deeds allowed one single-family house plus outbuildings "appropriate for a gentleman's country residence." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Vose died by suicide (drowning) at Atlantic City on March 17, 1874. The Panic of 1873 may have added to his troubles. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Village-website version of Vose's timeline: in 1858 he "bought a home on Scotland Road," and in 1862 he began buying large plots; "within a few years 175 acres between Scotland Road and Center Street were developed. He named the area Montrose." — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- **George B. Turrell.** Turrell came in 1864 and bought the ~11-acre D.J. Sprague property on Scotland Street, between Raymond and Irving Avenues and running through to Grove Road. In 1891 he opened Turrell Avenue and subdivided the property. He was Village President in 1871 and again in 1873, when he introduced "Turrell pavement." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **Kingman era (1891–1903).**
  - Thomas Sewall Kingman (1840–1903) was a dry-goods executive at Jordan Marsh, then A.T. Stewart, then Brown, Wood and Kingman. He retired in 1892 to pursue real estate.
  - About 1886 he bought the ~40-acre Aaron Bishop Baldwin farm, bounded west by Centre Street, south by South Orange Avenue, and east by St. Mary's Orphanage (Holland Road).
  - In 1891 he formed a syndicate with minority partners Henry Finlay and William F. Havemeyer (spelled "Havermeyer" in the NR), named the tract "Montrose Park," and laid out Hartford Road, Stanley Road (named for explorer Henry M. Stanley), and Kingman Road.
  - Centre Street was "graded, curbed and flagged" as the main thoroughfare. Lots averaged 100×200 ft, and about 40 houses cost $6,000–$45,000 each. His own house is 457 Centre Street. His holdings eventually reached ~150 acres.

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The land east of Centre Street was annexed to the Village by an act approved February 10, 1891. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd); the SOPL timeline gives "Montrose Park, 150 acres, annexed ... February 10, 1891." — [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline)
- After Kingman died (Oct 10, 1903), Anna H. Kingman's name appears on many lots in the 1904 Mueller atlas. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **The 1920s Seton Hall farm tract.** Seton Hall sold its last 15-acre farm tract, at the NW corner of South Orange Avenue and Centre Street, in 1922. By 1928 it was fully built with University, Fielding, Elm, Warren, and Marshall Courts (Robinson 1928, Plate 11). It covers Blocks 902–904, the triangle of South Orange Avenue, University Court, and Turrell Avenue. Lots are smaller there, and the streets curve. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd); [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- **Later estate subdivisions.**
  - Hartford Court was the drive to Kingman's first house and was built up by the end of WWII with smaller Colonial Revivals.
  - Henderson Drive, Thacher Lane, and Halsey Place were cut from the John Watson estate.
  - Connett Place was the entrance to the 6+ acre Connett estate on Scotland Road.

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Village population: 2,178 in 1880; 4,608 in 1890; 7,274 in 1920; 13,630 in 1930. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **Named estates/houses for house stories.**
  - "Aldworth": the Old Stone House, bought and enlarged by William A. Brewer Jr. in 1867.
  - "Holmwood": the Henry M. Keasbey estate.
  - W.V. Brokaw estate, later the Pauline Theriot estate, on Scotland Road (c.1890).
  - Belle H. March estate on Scotland Road.
  - The Katherine Kip house, 432 Scotland Road, which became Temple Israel in 1948.
  - The Elizabeth D. Hitch estate on Irving Avenue.
  - William F. Allen (inventor of Standard Time), who built a villa on Scotland Street in 1886 on the site of today's Oheb Shalom.

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **Street-name origins.**
  - Vose Avenue: John Gorham Vose.
  - Kingman Road: Thomas Kingman.
  - Halsey Place: Henry B. Halsey, a lumber and coal dealer.
  - Turrell Avenue: George Turrell.
  - Connett Place: Eugene Connett, Village President 1894–96.
  - Fielding Court: Charles Fielding.
  - Self Place: Edward Self, Village President 1888–89, whose farm near Seton Hall supplied dairy.
  - Coudert Place: William Coudert, the first Village President.
  - College Place, Seton Place, and University Court: Seton Hall.

  — [Patch, Montrose Park & Seton Hall streets](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/how-did-montrose-park-and-seton-hall-area-streets-get-named)
- CONFLICT on George Turrell's business: NR says he was "a successful hardware manufacturer from Connecticut and New York City." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). Patch says he "created a way to bottle and ship milk within 20 minutes of milking." — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/how-did-montrose-park-and-seton-hall-area-streets-get-named)

### Cited Findings: styles by era and street (for house stories)
- Vose-era houses (c.1860s–1870s): 140 Montrose Ave is Italianate (Thayer family), and 169 Charlton Ave is French Second Empire (part of the J. Mitchell Gould estate). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The 1880s were "dominated by" Shingle Style and Queen Anne. Examples:
  - Shingle: 584 Hamilton Rd; 322 and 358 Hartford Rd; 218 Irving Ave; 45 Kingman Rd; 349/358/379 Montrose Ave; 61/62/66/70/147 Ralston Ave; 112/128/158/251 Raymond Ave; 102/281/314/361/425 Scotland Rd; 139/217/240 Turrell Ave; 269 and 425 Vose Ave.
  - Queen Anne: 351 Hartford Rd; 44 Kingman Rd; 406 Montrose Ave; 71 and 163 Ralston Ave; 378 Turrell Ave; 204 Vose Ave.

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The 1890s (Kingman syndicate):
  - East of Centre Street: Shingle (45 Kingman Rd, 584 Hamilton Rd) and Colonial Revival (18/28/40 Kingman Rd, 578/583 Hamilton Rd).
  - Hartford Road is predominantly Colonial Revival.
  - Tudor Revival came later and predominates on Centre Street, Grove Road, Charlton Avenue, and Irving Avenue (e.g., 152/176/363/394 Charlton; 423 Centre; 302/366 Grove; 138/151/162 Irving; 135 Turrell).

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- District-wide frequency: Colonial Revival (Georgian, Adam, and Dutch Colonial influences dominant), then Shingle; then, in descending order, Tudor Revival, Queen Anne, Italian Renaissance Revival, Italianate, French Second Empire, Mission, Romanesque Revival, French eclectic, medievalizing, Art Deco, and Gothic Revival. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Streetscape: "wide, curved streets ... lined with bluestone sidewalks and curbs, Belgian block gutters ... lit by Victorian gaslight." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Lot sizes: "Many of these properties north of Irving Avenue, as well as Centre Street, Stanley Road, and Kingman Road are a half-acre or larger." Lots are narrower south of Grove Road and Irving Avenue. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

### Inferences
- House story template, by location within Montrose Park:
  - West of Centre Street on Montrose, Raymond, Ralston, Warwick, Charlton, Irving, or Grove: the land was likely Vose land (1860s–1873 plat). Earlier owners were the Graham, Deas, and Condit farms.
  - East of Centre Street (Kingman, Hartford, Stanley, Hamilton): the land was the Aaron Bishop Baldwin farm, became Kingman's 1891 "Montrose Park," and was annexed in 1891.
  - The University/Fielding/Elm/Warren/Marshall Courts: Seton Hall dairy farm, built 1922–28.
- Vose's middle name is "Gorham" in most places in the NR, in MP2021, and on the Village site. The NR's "Graham" (twice) is probably an OCR or typing error. Likewise Kingman's middle initial is "S." (Sewall); the NR's Section 7 "Thomas A. Kingman" is likely an error.

- The NR nomination's figures (Taylor 1873 map, atlas plates) are reproduced only as images; plat book/page numbers for filed maps at the Essex County Register were not found.
- I did not find the developers or builders of the 1920s Seton Hall farm courts.

## 3. Other nineteenth-century neighborhoods: Academy Heights/Prospect Street, Seton Village, West Montrose, South Mountain–South Ridgewood, North Ridgewood Road/Lower Wyoming, and the Village center

### Takeaway
These sections developed in the following order:
- **Academy Heights**, south of the station: cottages from the late 1830s–1840s, and Luther Milligan's larger houses of 1871.
- **Seton Village**: streets between South Orange Avenue and Irvington Avenue laid out 1870–1900.
- **West Montrose**: Meeker Street just after the Civil War, Hillside Place after 1880.
- **North Ridgewood Road**: large estates from the late 1800s to c.1910, on the line of the colonial Brown, Squier, and Redmond farms.
- **South Ridgewood Road**: early-Victorian Gothic houses, plus the Fenner family houses on Walton Avenue.

### Cited Findings
**Academy Heights / Prospect Street Historic District** (center approx. 40.743, -74.259. Representative OSM points: Second Street 40.7444, -74.2609; Milligan Place 40.7439, -74.2574; Prospect Street 40.7448, -74.2557; Roland Avenue 40.7379, -74.2617. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Milligan+Place,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "The earliest residential development outside of downtown occurred in Academy Heights, where modest wood-frame cottages were built as early as the late 1830s and early 1840s." Cottages sit on lots 30–50 ft wide. Their plans were likely from pattern books, with some Italianate and Stick elements. The most distinctive are on Second Street between Valley and Prospect Streets. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- "Larger, more elegant homes were built after the Civil War, including those developed by Luther Milligan in 1871, which survive today on Milligan Place and Second Street." Larger lots on Third Street, Prospect Street, and Milligan Place hold Second Empire, Italianate, and Stick houses, some now institutional (including the Elks). Our Lady of Sorrows church dates to c.1929. Notable houses: the Stick villa at 152 Prospect and the compact Gothic house at 206 Prospect. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- "The southern part of Academy Heights was developed later than the northern section." It includes the Roland Avenue cottages and 225, 237, and 319 Prospect. These houses "constitute the core of the Prospect Street Historic District." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- In 1860 the most densely settled part of the village "extended from the railroad to Prospect Street, and from South Orange Avenue to Fourth Street." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- The Columbian School's forerunner was a one-room stone schoolhouse on "the Common," near the junction of South Orange Avenue and Academy Street. A planned toll road threatened the site, so a two-story wooden school was built nearby for 1815. A brick school at South Orange Avenue and Academy Street served from 1880 to 1927. — [Matters Magazine, "From School House to High School" (search snippet; not fetched)](https://www.mattersmagazine.com/post/from-school-house-to-high-school-by-adrianna-donat). The SOPL timeline dates the Columbian School to c.1814–1815. — [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline)

**Seton Village (Cottage Street to Ward Place)** (center approx. 40.743, -74.253. OSM points: Riggs Place 40.7435, -74.2529; Cottage Street 40.7440, -74.2543; Fairview Avenue 40.7430, -74.2517; Ward Place 40.7392, -74.2540. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Riggs+Place,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "This section of Seton Village was built up between 1870 and 1900 with the laying out of streets — Cottage Street, Riggs Place, and Fairview Avenue — that connected South Orange and Irvington Avenues."
  - Lots are among the narrowest in town (some on Riggs Place only 25 ft wide) but 150–175 ft deep.
  - Small wood-frame houses built on Cottage Street in the early 1870s "presumably gave the street its name."
  - Riggs Place, developed later, has Queen Anne and Colonial houses. Fairview Avenue has modest Queen Anne and Colonial Revival houses.
  - The part of Seton Village south of the university was "built mostly in the early twentieth century."

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The Ward Homestead at 154 Ward Place "may date to" the Revolutionary era; reports range from 1770 to 1815. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Riggs Place is mapped as a potentially NR-eligible "Riggs Place Historic District." — [HD-Map](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/246/Historic-Districts-PDF-)

**West Montrose (Meeker Street and Hillside Place)** (center approx. 40.754, -74.256. OSM points: Meeker Street 40.7539, -74.2551; Hillside Place 40.7555, -74.2580; Mountain Station 40.7553, -74.2528. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Meeker+Street,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "West Montrose was first developed in the years following the Civil War. The first structures appeared on Meeker Street."
  - 19th-century houses line Meeker Street from Montrose Avenue to Mountain House Road, including two Second Empire and three Stick houses (430 Meeker is Second Empire).
  - 52 West Montrose Avenue is a notable Queen Anne.
  - Lots are about 50×145 ft. Stick houses on Church and Third Streets are often altered.
  - "Much of the rest of West Montrose was developed after 1880." Hillside Place has Queen Anne, Shingle, Colonial Revival (e.g., 349 Hillside Pl), and Mediterranean Revival houses.

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Pre-suburban and estate history for the West Montrose area:
  - Edwin H. Mead (b. 1822, Pittsfield MA; president and treasurer of the Pennsylvania Coal Co.; Village President) bought 10 acres at Ridgewood Road and Vose Avenue, his estate "Springlawn."
  - Mead Street is named for him. Winthrop Terrace is named for his son Winthrop. Clark Street is named for Village President Daniel T. Clark. Meeker Street is named for the Meeker family. Mountain House Road is named for the Mountain House spa.

  — [Patch, West Montrose streets](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/how-did-west-montrose-streets-get-named)
- In 1889 the Meadow Land Society, led by William F. Allen, bought 23 acres from Edwin H. Mead to keep industry out of the valley between the Lackawanna railroad and Ridgewood Road. That land became the South Orange Field Club (1889). Mead's barn served as its clubhouse until it burned in Jan 1895. The site is now Meadowland Park, and the clubhouse is now the Baird Community Center. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Draining of the East Branch of the Rahway River (1882–84) reclaimed "a large area of swamp land" west of Montrose. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Note: the NR uses "Valley Road (now Clark Street)." That is a different road from today's Valley Street. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)

**South Mountain – South Ridgewood Road (19th-century layer)** (OSM points: Walton Avenue 40.7426, -74.2680; West End Road 40.7455, -74.2717; Conway Court 40.7471, -74.2656. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Walton+Avenue,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "South Ridgewood Road has several distinctive houses from the early Victorian era." These include Gothic-influenced houses at 24 and 184 South Ridgewood and a c.1850 Gothic Revival at 44 South Ridgewood Road. Houses at 206, 216, 224, and 291 Walton Avenue were "all built by the Fenner family, on a large tract of land they owned." Lots on South Ridgewood can be as narrow as 25–30 ft. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- 360 West End Road is listed as an isolated early vernacular house. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

**North Ridgewood Road + Lower Wyoming (north of Beech Spring Road)** (OSM points: Melrose Place 40.7593, -74.2615; Tillou Road 40.7503, -74.2656; Beech Spring Road 40.7512, -74.2648. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Tillou+Road,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- Colonial farms:
  - Brothers Joseph and Thomas Brown began a farm northwest of the junction of the "two old trails" (now South Orange Avenue and Ridgewood Road) in 1677. A 1686 survey places their house at the SW corner of Tillou Road and Ridgewood Road.
  - Joseph Riggs had a house at the NW corner of South Orange Avenue and Ridgewood Road, per a 1705 road survey.

  Both facts come from Wikipedia citing Shaw 1884. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey)
- "The brownstone first story was built by Henry Squier circa 1774" at 167 North Ridgewood Road (the Squier House, "Overlooking Floods Hill"). — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021). In 1850 William Redmond acquired it with the Squier farm. He built a brownstone mansion now used by the Orange Lawn Tennis Club. — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- In 1916 the Orange Lawn Tennis Club bought the Redmond estate "Hillside" on Ridgewood Road and moved there from Montrose. It had been in Montrose since 1880, on 10 acres of the Vose estate between Montrose and Warwick Avenues, where it hosted the 1886 National Championships. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- "The most distinctive of the homes on North Ridgewood Road were built in the late nineteenth or just after the turn of the twentieth century."
  - Styles: mostly Colonial Revival, less often Tudor Revival, with single examples of Queen Anne, Shingle, Craftsman/Tudor, Mediterranean villa, and Chateauesque.
  - Examples: 105 N Ridgewood (c.1905 Craftsman/Tudor); 305 N Ridgewood (the former William Redmond estate, now Orange Lawn Tennis Club); 282 Melrose Place (Colonial Revival); 320 N Ridgewood (Italian Renaissance Revival); 351 N Ridgewood ("Linden Lawn," French Renaissance Revival, c.1903); 407 N Ridgewood (eclectic); 324 N Ridgewood (Albert Moyer House, c.1910, Mediterranean-influenced eclectic, by Tracy & Swartwout, with Mercer tile).
  - "Lower Wyoming, Melrose Place and Glenside Road may be potential candidates for inclusion in an historic district."

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Isolated early vernacular houses in Lower Wyoming: 30 Hart Drive South and 520 North Wyoming Avenue. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The Mountain House resort (a water cure) stood on the west side of Ridgewood Road and accommodated 150 guests. It was owned in 1850 by "Mr. Lord of Lord and Taylor's" and burned Aug 23, 1890. Mountain Station and Mountain House Road are its remnants. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). Patch places it "near the corner of North Ridgewood Road and Glenside Road" and says it opened in 1830. — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/how-did-west-montrose-streets-get-named)
- CONFLICT on the Mountain House:
  - Opening date: "built around 1830" — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd) and [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline). "Established in 1847 on Ridgewood Road in present-day West Orange" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey).
  - Fire date: the Village site says "destroyed by fire after 1881." — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History)

**Village center / Central Business District (South Orange Avenue)** (South Orange station 40.7460, -74.2606. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=South+Orange+station,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- During the Revolution the village had "a few farmhouses, a stone schoolhouse, a blacksmith's shop, a gristmill, a general store, and a tavern." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021); [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline)
- "Most of the two- and three-story commercial structures that once lined South Orange Avenue have been demolished."
  - Surviving candidates: the Baum Building (originally the Cameo Movie Theater, 1920s); 14 South Orange Ave (historic storefront with Ionic pilasters); 18 South Orange Ave (original site of the Town Hall Deli, "birthplace of the 'sloppy joe'"); and the Art Deco Ridgewood Commons apartments at N Ridgewood Road and South Orange Avenue.
  - Other landmarks: the former Village Hall (1894, Rossiter and Wright); the French Eclectic fire station on Sloan Street (1925; NR March 19, 1998); the Romanesque Connett Library (1896); the Art Deco Post Office on Vose Ave (begun 1936, WPA, with a Bernard Perlin mural); and four churches on South Orange Avenue and Valley Street (First Baptist c.1913).

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The South Orange railroad station was built in 1916 and NR-listed in 1984. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey). Mountain Station was NR-listed 9/29/1984 (84002656). — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

### Inferences
- Approximate centers given above are my midpoints of representative street points, not official centroids.
- An address on Second Street, Milligan Place, Third Street, or Prospect Street south of the station can be told as South Orange's oldest suburb story: cottages from the 1830s–40s, Milligan's 1871 houses, and Victorian villas on Prospect.
- An address on Cottage Street, Riggs Place, or Fairview Avenue is a narrow-lot 1870–1900 "worker/middle-class" story, tied to the Seton Hall campus and the Irvington Avenue corridor.
- The "Riggs" of Riggs Place may honor the colonial Riggs family (Joseph Riggs, 1705). That is not confirmed, and his house was at a different location (South Orange Avenue and Ridgewood Road).

### Gaps
- No source found for who laid out Cottage Street, Riggs Place, or Fairview Avenue, or for Luther Milligan's background.
- No text found for the Prospect Street HD SHPO opinion (date, boundaries).

## 4. Twentieth-century neighborhoods: Tuxedo Park, Lower Wyoming South, Upper Wyoming, South Mountain (Tudor), Newstead

### Takeaway
Tuxedo Park, on the eastern edge near Seton Hall, was built on Thomas D. Kilburn's former land. Its first houses went up in 1911, it was formally organized in 1913, and most of it was built before WWI. Lower Wyoming's southern part (Tillou, Beech Spring, Highland) dates to 1910–1930. Upper Wyoming (Mayhew, Harding, Twin Oak, Overhill) is 1920s–early 1930s Tudor and Colonial Revival. South Mountain is 1920s–30s Tudor. Newstead on the mountaintop was last: a few Tudors and Colonials from the late 1920s and early 1930s, then mostly post-WWII ranches and split-levels. George H. Becker developed many Newstead homes.

### Cited Findings
**Tuxedo Park** (OSM point: Cameron Road 40.7429, -74.2404; Seton Hall University 40.7433, -74.2469. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Cameron+Road,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "Orange's Thomas D. Kilburn" first owned the whole tract. The first houses were built in 1911, and it was "officially organized" as a neighborhood in 1913, followed by slow, steady growth. It lies near Seton Hall University on South Orange's eastern border. The name's origin is unknown; it may be borrowed from Tuxedo Park, NY. — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/tuxedo-park-marks-a-century)
- Thomas D. Kilburn appears on the 1860 map as a major landowner east of Centre Street. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- "Tuxedo Park was constructed mostly during a short period prior to World War I."
  - Lots north of Cameron Road are 60–80 ft wide by ~150 ft deep. South of Cameron Road most are 50 ft or less by 100 ft.
  - "Architecturally, the neighborhood stands out for its Mediterranean Revival homes," with bungalows, Colonial Revival, Arts & Crafts-influenced, and Tudor houses.
  - "Earlier studies have identified Tuxedo Park as an area to survey for the potential designation of a historic district."

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Cameron Road and Cameron Field are named for Rev. Lewis Cameron of St. Andrew's Church of the Holy Communion, who gave the Village land for a playground (Cameron Field). — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/how-did-montrose-park-and-seton-hall-area-streets-get-named). A search summary of the SOPL timeline dated Cameron Field to 1912. That date did not appear in my direct fetch of the timeline, so treat it as unverified. — [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline)

**Lower Wyoming South (Tillou, Beech Spring, Highland Roads)**
- "Lower Wyoming's southern portion was built largely between 1910 and 1930." The Tillou Road lots are the largest (100×250 ft), and its houses are the oldest in this area. Most houses here are Tudor and Colonial Revival. 279 Tillou Road is Art Moderne with glass block and was identified as a potential historic site in the 1986 Essex County survey. 375 Redmond Road is Colonial Revival. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The Lower Wyoming lots along Highland Road, near the West Orange line, are often non-conforming (small). — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

**Upper Wyoming** (OSM points: Harding Drive 40.7529, -74.2715; Mayhew Drive 40.7512, -74.2712; Twin Oak Road 40.7512, -74.2710. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Harding+Drive,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "Tudor and Colonial Revival homes built in the 1920s and early 1930s account for much of Upper Wyoming's housing stock."
  - The largest concentration is on Mayhew Road and Harding Drive near Overhill and Twin Oak Roads. The "most ornate" are at 472–496 Mayhew Court. 364 Harding Drive is a Tudor example.
  - Lots on north–south streets (Mayhew Ct, Mayhew Rd, Harding Dr) are about 75 ft wide; on east–west streets (Twin Oak) about 100 ft.

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Upper and Lower Wyoming have "a significant number of half-acre or larger residential lots, particularly along North Ridgewood Road, North Wyoming Avenue, and Harding Drive." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- The Village site names Turrell, Kingman, Connett, Mead, Speir, and Mayhew as businessmen who "bought farms, laid out streets." — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History). F.L.B. (Francis Le Baron) Mayhew and Francis Speir Jr. were Village Presidents, and Mayhew was among the library founders in 1864. — [Schnell 1937](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/The_Village_of_South_Orange:_A_Brief_History_by_Harry_Schnell)

**South Mountain (20th-century layer)**
- "The housing stock in South Mountain consists largely of Tudor houses built in the 1920s and 30s." The most notable are on West End Road and Western Avenue, with a smaller Tudor pocket on Conway Court, which connects South Orange Avenue and South Ridgewood Road. 342–348 South Ridgewood Road, an unusual fieldstone-and-brick classical house, is now condominiums. Lot widths are 60–75 ft. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

**Newstead** (OSM points: Glenview Road 40.7593, -74.2744; Longview Road 40.7568, -74.2709; Hoskier Road 40.7546, -74.2769. Approx. center 40.757, -74.275. — [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Glenview+Road,+South+Orange,+NJ&format=json))
- "The Village's western-most neighborhood, Newstead, was the last to be developed. The Tudor and Colonial homes of Newstead first appeared in the late 1920s and early 1930s, and development began in earnest after World War II. George H. Becker developed many homes [in] the Newstead neighborhood."
  - The Tudors and Colonials are mostly in the southern section. The northern area is distinguished by "impressive mid-century architecture including many impressive ranch and split-level style homes" (e.g., the ranch at 625 Longview Road).
  - It is suggested for a future mid-century historic district survey.
  - Its lots are the widest in the Village, up to 150 ft.

  — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Newstead sits at the westernmost edge, on top of South Mountain. Its "entrance" is at Glenview Road and South Orange Avenue, about 1.4 miles downhill from the station. Some lots back onto the South Mountain Reservation. "Most of the homes being built during the 1950's." — [Village Green (2015)](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=19121)
- A George H. Becker is listed among South Orange Village Presidents. — [Schnell 1937](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/The_Village_of_South_Orange:_A_Brief_History_by_Harry_Schnell)
- Overall build-out: "Developers platted most of the remaining land in the Village by the Great Depression"; the Village was "effectively 'built out' by 1950." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)

### Inferences
- The Newstead developer George H. Becker and the Village President George H. Becker may be the same person, but no source links them.
- Mayhew Road, Mayhew Drive, and Mayhew Court in Upper Wyoming are probably named for the Mayhew family (F.L.B. Mayhew), and Tillou Road for the Tillou family (Job Tillou was an 1860 landowner). Both are unconfirmed.
- "Wyoming" in South Orange most likely takes its name from Wyoming Avenue and the adjoining Wyoming section of Millburn. A Millburn–Short Hills Historical Society volume says that section was named after a visitor likened it to Pennsylvania's Wyoming Valley. — [MSHHS Vol. 27 (search snippet; not fetched)](https://mshhistsoc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Vol27.pdf). This is inference only.
- The MP2021 statement that Tuxedo Park is "mostly ... prior to World War I" and Patch's "slow but steady" growth after 1913 are compatible, but the bungalows and Tudors suggest some 1920s infill.

### Gaps
- No source names the developers or land company of Tuxedo Park, Upper Wyoming, Lower Wyoming, or South Mountain.
- No source names Newstead's pre-development owner or estate, or the origin of the name "Newstead." Hoskier Road suggests a Hoskier estate, but that is unverified.
- Exact boundaries of Tuxedo Park, Upper/Lower Wyoming, South Mountain, and Newstead were not found in text.

## 5. Developers, builders, and architects active in South Orange (with dates)

### Takeaway
The documented developers are:
- John Gorham Vose and Henry A. Page (Montrose, 1860s–1874)
- George B. Turrell (Turrell Ave, 1891)
- Thomas S. Kingman's syndicate with Henry Finlay and William F. Havemeyer (Montrose Park, 1891 on)
- Lewis P. Taylor and G.W. Comstock (Taylor/Comstock Places, 1873 map)
- Luther Milligan (Academy Heights, 1871)
- the Fenner family (Walton Ave)
- George H. Becker (Newstead, mid-20th c.)

The documented architects are Rossiter & Wright, Guilbert & Betelle, Tracy & Swartwout, G.W. Maher, Dillon McLellan & Beadel, Davis McGrath & Kiessling, Stanley & Wheeler, and William E. Lehman (as a resident). No South Orange work by Kenneth Dalzell or Edward Balch was found.

### Inferences
- "G.W. Maher" may be the Chicago Prairie School architect George W. Maher. The NR gives only initials, so this is unconfirmed.
- Montrose School is on Clark Street per Schnell 1937, which places it in West Montrose, not inside the Montrose Park HD. — [Schnell 1937](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/The_Village_of_South_Orange:_A_Brief_History_by_Harry_Schnell)

### Gaps
- Kenneth Dalzell and Edward Balch: no South Orange commissions were found in any source consulted (NR-Montrose, MP2021). Treat any claim of their South Orange work as unverified.
- Speculative builders for 1910s–1950s subdivisions (Tuxedo Park, Wyoming, Newstead beyond Becker) are undocumented in the sources found.
- The South Orange Historical & Preservation Society ([sohps.org](https://www.sohps.org)) was identified but not mined. Its house-tour booklets would likely name architects and builders.

## 6. Typical architectural styles and eras, village-wide

### Takeaway
About 85% of South Orange's houses date from the late 1800s to the 1930s. Typical styles by era:
- Late 1830s–1860s: vernacular, pattern-book, and Gothic cottages.
- 1860s–1870s: Italianate and Second Empire.
- 1880s: Shingle and Queen Anne, plus Stick.
- 1890s–1910s: Colonial Revival and Tudor.
- 1910s–1930s: Mediterranean Revival, bungalow, Tudor, and Colonial Revival.
- 1930s–1950s: Art Moderne, ranch, and split-level.

## 7. Historic roads: origins, routes, and dates

### Takeaway
- **South Orange Avenue**: a Native American trail. It became the Newark and Morris Turnpike (chartered Feb 11, 1811) and carried a horse-car line by 1870, electrified by 1893.
- **Ridgewood Road** (the "Upper Trail") and **Valley Street** (the "Lower Trail"): Native trails that led toward Newark Bay. They were among the "first primitive highways" under the 1705 road statutes.
- **Scotland Road, Montrose Avenue, and Centre Street**: existed by 1860.
- **Irvington Avenue and Prospect Street**: the 1834 village was concentrated along them and South Orange Avenue.
- **Vose's streets**: opened 1866–1874. **Kingman's streets** (Hartford, Stanley, Kingman): from 1891.
- **Wyoming Avenue**: a county road shared with Maplewood and Millburn.

### Cited Findings
- **South Orange Avenue.**
  - It was "an Indian trail" that "served as the main thoroughfare." — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History); "Originally an Indian trail," with alternate names "Third Road" and "South Orange Road." — [newarkstreets.com](https://newarkstreets.com/s/southorangeavenue.php)
  - Charter: "February 11th 1811 the Newark and Morris Turnpike Company was chartered," with incorporators John Doughty, Benjamin Pierson, Caleb Campbell, Seth Woodruff, Moses W. Combs, and Jabez Pierson. "The road was to pass through South Orange to Bottle Hill (Madison) or to Morristown." — [Munsell, History of Morris County, ch. 11](https://www.usgenwebsites.org/NJMorris/history/munsellhistory/h-chpt11.htm)
  - In 1834 South Orange "lies on the turnpike from Newark to Morristown." On the 1860 Hughes map it is labeled "the Newark Turnpike." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - Horse cars: the Newark, Montrose and South Orange Horse Car Railroad Co. (Vose, president) ran along South Orange Avenue between downtown Newark and South Orange, about 5 miles. It was one of seven horse-car routes from Newark by 1870, and it was electrified by 1893. Vose as Trustee oversaw paving of South Orange Avenue (1870–72). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - In 1814–15 a planned toll road forced the Columbian School to move from "the Common" at South Orange Avenue and Academy Street. — [Matters Magazine (search snippet; not fetched)](https://www.mattersmagazine.com/post/from-school-house-to-high-school-by-adrianna-donat)
  - Its junction with Ridgewood Road was the junction of "two old trails." The Brown farm lay NW of it in 1677, and Joseph Riggs's house stood at its NW corner in 1705. — [Wikipedia (citing Shaw 1884)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey)
- **Ridgewood Road.**
  - It "was but a trail winding through the hills and traversed only by Native Americans," called the Upper Trail. — [Durand-Hedden](https://www.durandhedden.org/post/maplewood-in-the-orange-mountains)
  - "1705: road statutes required landowners to maintain the first primitive highways. These included Main Street and Valley and Ridgewood roads." — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
  - It is the spine of the North Ridgewood estates (Squier 1774, Redmond, the Mountain House) and the South Ridgewood Victorian houses. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- **Valley Street.** "At the foot of the hill ran the Lower Trail, now Valley Street." Both the Upper and Lower Trails led to tidal waters now known as Newark Bay. In Maplewood, Valley Street once was "not cut through beyond Parker Avenue." — [Durand-Hedden](https://www.durandhedden.org/post/maplewood-in-the-orange-mountains)
- CAUTION: in the 1860s–70s "Valley Road" meant today's Clark Street. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). So the 1705 "Valley ... road" on the Village site could mean either road.
- **Scotland Road** (historically "Scotland Street").
  - It existed by 1860, with population concentrated on its east side. Vose's home was at the NE corner of Scotland and Montrose (about 460 Scotland Rd). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
  - Revolutionary-era lookouts were posted at "Baldwin's Station on Scotland Road." — [Durand-Hedden](https://www.durandhedden.org/post/maplewood-in-the-orange-mountains)
  - It is a county road today. — [Village Green (search snippet)](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=43236)
  - It was lined with large estates (Allen 1886, Minnott 1895, Brokaw/Theriot, March, Kip, Connett) and the Connett Library (1896, at Taylor Place). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **Centre Street.** It existed by 1860. It was the eastern edge of the 1869 Village; land east of it was annexed in 1891. It was graded, curbed, and flagged by Kingman as the "main thoroughfare" of Montrose Park (1891+). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- **Montrose Avenue.** It appears on the 1860 Hughes map and probably gave the neighborhood its name. Vose and Page laid out the section from Scotland Street to Valley Road (Clark St). Mountain Station stands "at the head of Montrose Avenue." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). In 1915 the grade crossing at Mountain Station was eliminated, with the roadway raised on a bridge. — [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline)
- **Irvington Avenue and Prospect Street.** In 1834 settlement was concentrated on South Orange Avenue, Prospect Street, and Irvington Avenue. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). There are early vernacular houses at 204 and 407 Irvington Avenue. Cottage Street, Riggs Place, and Fairview Avenue (1870–1900) "connected South Orange and Irvington Avenues." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- **Wyoming Avenue.** It runs through Maplewood, South Orange, and Millburn and is county-maintained. — [Patch, county Wyoming Ave repaving (search snippet)](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/county-completes-wyoming-avenue-repaving). South Mountain School stands at South Orange and Wyoming Avenues. — [Schnell 1937](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/The_Village_of_South_Orange:_A_Brief_History_by_Harry_Schnell)
- **Mountain House Road.** It was the road leading to the Mountain House hotel (c.1830–1890). — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- Today's arterials and collectors: Scotland Road, Valley Street, South Orange Avenue, Irvington Avenue, Prospect Street, North and South Wyoming Avenue, and North and South Ridgewood Road. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- Railroad: the Morris and Essex RR began in 1836 (a horse-drawn single track), extended in 1837, and reached Hoboken in 1868. By 1869 there were 15 trains daily each way. The tracks were elevated and Mountain Station built in 1915, the South Orange station in 1916, and the first electric train ran Sept 3, 1930. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd); [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline); [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- Historic map sources (useful for overlays):
  - Hughes, "Map of South Orange and Orange Valley," 1860
  - Lewis P. Taylor, "Map of South Orange & Montrose," May 1873
  - Pidgeon, Atlas of Essex County, 1881 (Plates 124–125, 128–129)
  - E. Robinson, Atlas of Essex County, 1890 (Plates 30, 34)
  - A.H. Mueller, Atlas of the Oranges, 1904 and 1911 (Plates 21–22)
  - E. Robinson, Atlas of Irvington, South Orange, Maplewood and Millburn, 1928 (Plates 10–11)

  — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd). A 1906 Mueller map exists for the Wyoming section of Millburn. — [MSHHS catalog](https://hub.catalogit.app/millburn-short-hills-historical-society/folder/entry/c7b00f60-2d2b-11eb-8afe-c36686951776)

### Inferences
- The user's premise ("1881 Hughes") is off. Hughes's South Orange map is dated 1860, and the 1881 atlas is Pidgeon's. The early-1900s Atlas of the Oranges editions used in the NR are 1904 and 1911, not 1906.
- East–west roads toward Newark and Newark Bay (South Orange Avenue/the turnpike, and the trail lines of Valley Street and Ridgewood Road) are the oldest. North–south streets such as Scotland, Centre, and Wyoming connected farms and later served subdivisions.

### Gaps
- No completion date, toll-gate locations, or end-of-tolls date was found for the Newark and Morris Turnpike.
- No sourced naming origin was found for Scotland Road, Centre Street, Irvington Avenue, or Wyoming Avenue.
- No date was found for when Wyoming Avenue or Irvington Avenue was laid out.

## 8. Gas lamps: history and which streets have them

### Takeaway
South Orange still has about 1,400–1,440 gas street lamps, owned by PSE&G. Most residential streets are gas-lit, including all of Montrose Park ("lit by Victorian gaslight"). Major roads and the downtown use electric lamps in gaslight-style frames. The start date of gas lighting is disputed: 1860, 1872 contracts, or the 1880s–90s.

### Cited Findings
- "Street lamps change over from burning sperm whale oil to a gas service" in 1860. — [SOPL-Timeline](https://localhistory.sopl.org/wiki/Historical_Timeline); "Street lamps in the town's center burned sperm oil until 1860 when gas service became available ... gas lines were laid in the 1890s." — [Village-History](https://southorange.org/342/History)
- Fall 1872: the Trustees entertained proposals for gas lighting. In November the Village was divided into three lighting districts, "Montrose, Mountain and Valley," and the first contract went to the Citizens Gas Light Company. — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- CONFLICT: MP2021 says "Workers installed the Village's signature gaslamps in the 1880s and 90s." — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- A 1970s proposal to replace all gas lamps with electric lights "was soundly rejected." The lamps' output was increased instead. — [MP2021](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/6166/South-Orange-Master-Plan---Adopted-Nov-2021)
- "Most residential areas are still gas-lit. Major roads have mercury vapor lights built into gaslight frames." A 2010 automation project covered 1,438 lamps. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey)
- Downtown streetscape (South Orange Avenue from Ridgewood Road to Prospect Street, Sloan Street, Valley Street, Vose Avenue, Scotland Road): "Electrified gaslights replaced the old cobra headlamps." — [South Orange Smart Growth Plan, June 2007 draft, Planning Context and History](https://www.southorange.org/DocumentCenter/View/253/Planning-Context-and-History-PDF-)
- Montrose Park HD streets are "lit by Victorian gaslight." — [NR-Montrose](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2b0c9ff9-6014-40a7-b3b7-c1ab7eede3dd)
- PSE&G owns all the gas lamps. — [Patch (search snippet)](https://patch.com/new-jersey/southorange/bright-idea-south-orange-help-pse-g-fix-gaslights). Residents and trustees have complained that as many as 25% of lamps may be too low or out at any one time. — [Village Green (search snippet)](https://villagegreennj.com/?p=43236)

### Inferences
- For a house story, assume a residential side street in any neighborhood is gas-lit unless it is an arterial (South Orange Ave, Valley St, Scotland Rd, Irvington Ave, Ridgewood Rd, Wyoming Ave, Prospect St). Arterials typically have electric lamps in gaslight-style housings.

### Gaps
- No street-by-street inventory of gas versus electric lamps was found. PSE&G or the Village DPW would hold it.
- The 1860, 1872, and 1880s–90s start dates cannot be reconciled from the available sources. 1860 may refer to the downtown only, 1872 to the first village-wide contract, and the 1880s–90s to the residential network build-out. That reading is inference only.
