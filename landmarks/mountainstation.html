Railroad station · 1870s; rebuilt 1915

Mountain Station

Montrose's own station, first built by its developers and rebuilt in 1915, with a waiting-room landscape in tile.

📍 449 Vose Avenue🏛 National Register 1984 · key property, Montrose Park HD✏️ Frank J. Nies or his staff

Mountain Station platform · Adam Moss / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 2.0

A developers' depot

John Gorham Vose and Henry A. Page, who laid out Montrose in 1867–74, "built the depot at Montrose station… at their own expense." The stop was first called Montrose. Telephones arrived here and at the South Orange station on December 6, 1879.

In 1901 the original station on the site handled 74 trains a day.

The 1915 station

The present Renaissance Revival station dates from 1915, by Frank J. Nies or his staff. (The Montrose Park nomination says c. 1910.) It is the only place where the Lackawanna did not change the level of the roadbed, so Montrose Avenue was raised onto a bridge instead.

Over the water fountain in the main waiting room is a tile panel of a forest scene with a stream, hills and a waterfall, meant "to recall a Tiffany-like art glass window."

Did you know?

  • Traffic peaked at 96 trains a day in 1940.
  • It is one of the 40 key properties of the Montrose Park Historic District.

Fire-insurance maps

Mountain Station on the Sanborn maps

Sanborn maps were drawn for fire insurers and show every building: yellow for wood frame, pink for brick, blue for stone, with porches, stories and outbuildings. We matched this address to the sheet in each volume's street index. House numbers mostly match today's, but check the numbers written along the street.
